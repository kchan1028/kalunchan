KC fits roles where a leader needs to set technical direction and still ship: Director, Head or VP of Engineering, and Staff or Principal positions. Role scopes vary by company; the table below describes typical scope.

| Staff / Principal | Architecture and technical direction across teams, still hands-on | 23+ years hands-on, written architecture decisions, distributed systems |
| Consulting (via Yippify) | Scoped project, architecture review or delivery rescue | Web and mobile delivery for government, startup and founder clients |

## How does a founder-CTO background differ from a career engineering manager's?

A founder-CTO has owned every layer at once: product, architecture, hiring, operations and business outcomes. A career manager has usually worked within an established organization and processes. KC has done both: he built from inception as a co-founder and ran established functions as CTO and VP of Operations. That combination suits companies that need structure without losing speed.

## How does he lead engineering teams?

KC's guiding principle is to remove single points of failure. That covers systems, knowledge and decision-makers. In practice:

- **Turn ambiguity into executable work** before the team commits.
- **Make architecture decisions in writing**, so the reasoning outlasts the meeting.
- **Balance delivery and technical debt** explicitly, instead of letting debt pile up unseen.
- **Improve execution with small, safe changes:** small pull requests reviewed within a day, frequent deploys, and rollbacks that are rare and fast.
- **Reduce delivery risk early**, and build systems the next team can own.
- **Grow engineers into leaders**, and work closely across product, QA and leadership.

## What problems is he brought in to solve?

Teams bring KC in when delivery is slow or unpredictable, when the architecture no longer fits the product, or when too much depends on one person or system. Typical situations:

- A new product that needs to go from idea to production.
- A platform that has to scale past its original design.
- A team without clear technical direction or engineering leadership.
- Technical debt that is slowing every release.

Full-time pay for engineering leaders is negotiated and varies widely by level and company. Consulting is usually priced per project or by the hour.
